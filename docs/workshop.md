# Workshop: from motion to a measurable mechanism

**Suggested format:** two sessions, with printing and adhesive dwell between them. Use an unpowered training switch. Teams of two or three can share a mechanism, calipers and a force gauge.

## Session 1 · Understand and fit (90–120 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 15 min | Trace motion and load path in Fusion | Annotated sketch |
| 20 min | Measure switch, servo and horn | Completed measurement table |
| 20 min | Inspect coupon and fit printed guides | Pilot choice and clearance notes |
| 25 min | Assemble frame and X/Y adjustment | Photo at three rocker positions |
| 20 min | Characterize primary/return springs | Force-displacement table |
| 10 min | Decide whether the full print is ready | Signed gate checklist |

Discuss why a return spring is different from a spring in the force path. Predict what happens if either spring reaches solid height, if the tape peels at one corner, or if the cam is installed 90° out of phase.

## Between sessions

Print revised parts, deburr moving surfaces, run manual fit checks, and test the selected tape on representative plastic. Allow the tape's specified dwell time; do not compress this step to fit the class schedule.

## Session 2 · Assemble and test (90–120 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 25 min | Assemble plungers, cam and cradle | Assembly photograph and spring IDs |
| 20 min | Manual actuation on training switch | Force, travel and neutral clearance |
| 15 min | Inspect external low-voltage wiring | Polarity and pin-order checklist |
| 20 min | Calibrate small-angle powered motion | Angle/current/click record |
| 20 min | Run 20 alternating cycles | Pass/fail table and observations |
| 15 min | Propose the next design revision | One change justified by measurements |

## Design challenges

1. Reduce projection while preserving access to screws and springs.
2. Create a removable guard that cannot touch the rotating cam.
3. Design a manual bypass that still works after power loss.
4. Improve adhesive load distribution without using the wall or switch fixing screws.
5. Support another rocker spacing with matched guides and cam tracks.

Success means explaining a measured result and making a defensible improvement—not simply getting the servo to move.
